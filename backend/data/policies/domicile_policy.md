# Domicile Certificate — Official Eligibility Policy Corpus

**Source:** Maharashtra Government Gazette — General Administration Department
**Reference:** GR No. GAD-2019/CR-15/GAD-12, dated 15th March 2019
**Portal:** mahaonline.gov.in / Revenue Department, Government of Maharashtra

---

## Chapter 1 — Certificate Overview

### Section 1.1 — Definition
A Domicile Certificate (also called Residence Certificate) is an official government document issued by the competent revenue authority certifying that the person named therein is a permanent resident/domicile of the State of Maharashtra (or relevant state).

### Section 1.2 — Issuing Authority
- Sub-Divisional Magistrate (SDM) / Tehsildar of the applicant's residential jurisdiction.
- In urban areas: City Revenue Officer / SDM office.
- In rural areas: Tehsildar / Naib-Tehsildar.

### Section 1.3 — Legal Basis
- Maharashtra Regional and Town Planning Act, 1966.
- Bombay Tenancy and Agricultural Lands Act (for rural domiciles).
- Citizenship Act, 1955 (Section 5) — defining domicile of residence.

---

## Chapter 2 — Applicant Eligibility Criteria

### Section 2.1 — Citizenship Requirement
The applicant must be an Indian Citizen as defined under the Citizenship Act, 1955.

### Section 2.2 — Minimum Age
- Applicant must be 18 years or above at the time of application.
- For minors: Application must be filed by a parent or legal guardian; the certificate is issued in the minor's name.

### Section 2.3 — State of Domicile
The applicant can obtain a Domicile Certificate only for the state where they have their primary and continuous place of residence. A person cannot hold simultaneous domicile certificates for multiple states.

---

## Chapter 3 — Residency Duration Requirements

### Section 3.1 — Primary Residency Rule
**The applicant must have resided continuously in Maharashtra for a minimum of 15 (fifteen) years** from the date of application.

> **Clause 3.1.1 — Continuous Residency Definition:**
> Continuous residency means the applicant's primary domicile and habitual abode is in Maharashtra. Short absences (educational, employment, medical treatment) of up to 6 months per year do not break continuity.

### Section 3.2 — Proof of Residency
The applicant must submit documentary evidence showing residency for the required duration. Acceptable documents include:
- Property Tax receipts (oldest available, dated at least 15 years ago)
- Old Ration Card (issued 15+ years ago)
- School/College Leaving Certificate with Maharashtra address
- Old Electricity Bills (utility connection established 15+ years ago)
- Bank Passbook showing Maharashtra address for 15+ years
- Voter ID card (enrolled in Maharashtra constituency for 15+ years)

### Section 3.3 — Duration Verification Methodology
The Revenue authority verifies residency duration by:
1. Cross-referencing the oldest dated document with the date of application.
2. Verifying the address on residency documents matches the declared address.
3. Cross-referencing with municipal records (property tax / electoral rolls).

## Chapter 4 — Exemptions and Special Categories

### Section 4.1 — Government Employee Transfer Exemption
A government employee (central/state) transferred to Maharashtra is eligible for domicile after residing for **5 years** in the state (reduced from 15 years).

**Eligibility criteria:**
- Must have a valid transfer order from a recognized government department.
- Must have resided continuously in Maharashtra for at least 5 years from transfer date.
- Must submit service book / transfer order as proof.

### Section 4.2 — Educational Exemption (10-Year Schooling Rule)
**An applicant who has completed their entire primary and secondary education (Standard 1 to Standard 10) within Maharashtra is eligible for a domicile certificate after 10 years of residence** (reduced from the standard 15 years).

> **Clause 4.2.1 — Qualifying Conditions:**
> - Must have attended a recognized school within Maharashtra from Standard 1 (Class 1) to Standard 10 (SSC).
> - School Leaving Certificate / SSC/HSC Certificate from a Maharashtra State Board school must be submitted as proof.
> - The 10-year residency is calculated from the earliest year of schooling.

> **Clause 4.2.2 — Partial Education:**
> If the applicant completed only part of schooling in Maharashtra (e.g., transferred from another state mid-schooling), they do not qualify for the 10-year exemption. Standard 15-year rule applies.

### Section 4.3 — Born-in-Maharashtra Exemption
A person born in Maharashtra who has resided continuously in the state for at least 5 years at the time of application is eligible.

**Eligibility criteria:**
- Birth Certificate issued by a Maharashtra municipal body / Registrar of Births required.
- Must be able to prove 5-year continuous residence.

### Section 4.4 — Spouse of Domicile Holder
The spouse of a Maharashtra domicile holder is eligible for a Maharashtra domicile certificate after residing in Maharashtra for **3 years**, provided they submit:
- Marriage certificate.
- Spouse's Domicile Certificate.
- Own proof of 3-year residence in Maharashtra.

## Chapter 5 — Required Documents

### Section 5.1 — Identity Proof
- Aadhaar Card (mandatory) — must show Maharashtra address.
- Passport / Voter ID (supporting).

### Section 5.2 — Address Proof (Current Residence)
One of:
- Aadhaar Card with current Maharashtra address.
- Voter ID (Maharashtra constituency).
- Electricity / Water / Gas Bill (within last 3 months, Maharashtra service address).
- Ration Card (current Maharashtra address).
- Bank Passbook with Maharashtra address.
- Registered Rent Agreement.

### Section 5.3 — Residency Duration Proof
One of:
- Property Tax receipts covering required duration.
- Old Ration Card (15+ years old for general category).
- School Leaving Certificate from Maharashtra institution.
- Bank Passbook with earliest Maharashtra address entry.

### Section 5.4 — Additional Documents for Exemption Categories
- Clause 4.1 (Government Employee): Transfer order + Service book.
- Clause 4.2 (Educational): Maharashtra SSC/HSC School Leaving Certificate.
- Clause 4.3 (Born in Maharashtra): Birth certificate (Maharashtra).
- Clause 4.4 (Spouse): Marriage certificate + spouse's domicile certificate.

## Chapter 6 — Verification and Decision Process

### Section 6.1 — Document Verification
All documents submitted must be:
1. Self-attested (signed copy of original).
2. In good legible condition (no torn, faded, or tampered documents).
3. Cross-verified against Aadhaar database for name and address consistency.

### Section 6.2 — Field Verification
For borderline or suspicious applications, the Tehsildar's office may conduct a field verification visit to the declared residential address.

### Section 6.3 — Decision Timeline
- Auto-approved applications: Certificate generated within 7 working days.
- Officer review cases: Decision within 15 working days.
- Rejected applications: Rejection letter with reason issued within 7 working days.

### Section 6.4 — Grounds for Rejection
Applications shall be rejected if:
- Declared residence duration is less than required (15 years / 10 years / 5 years as applicable).
- Name or address on submitted documents does not match Aadhaar.
- Declared state of domicile does not match Aadhaar address.
- A valid Domicile Certificate for the same applicant and same state already exists.
- Fraudulent, forged, or tampered documents are detected.

### Section 6.5 — Officer Escalation Criteria
Applications are escalated to Human Officer Review if:
- System confidence score is below 95%.
- Any identity mismatch flag is raised.
- Documents are ambiguous, low quality, or OCR extraction fails.
- Exemption clause (4.1 / 4.2 / 4.3 / 4.4) is being claimed but cannot be auto-verified.

---

## Chapter 7 — Fees and Application Process

### Section 7.1 — Application Fee
- Category A (General): Rs. 50 per application.
- Category B (BPL / SC / ST): Nil.

### Section 7.2 — Validity
- The Domicile Certificate is valid for **3 years** from the date of issue.
- Renewal requires re-verification of current residence.
